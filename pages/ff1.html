Final Fantasy (2021) Full Review

Story: 3/5

The story of FFI is one that will go to be considered the "classic" JRPG story for years and years to come: a party of 4 "warriors of light" that go to restore order to 4 courrupted crystals, and defeat the main antagonist who caused all of the calamity to begin with. This on its own is plenty serviceable as a general overview, and the sidequests you go on as a group. The primary drawback being is that none of these sidequests are very fleshed out, and are not cohesive. An example is the fact that missions (such as the mermaid rescue mission) are not interesting enough and are barely preceded with any exposition or concluded with any interactions with NPCs (that I found) to signify the beneficial results of the quest. Yes, you can interact with memaids on the final floor of the dungeon, but the key piece being that the party is here primarily to defeat one of the 4 fiends...not save the mermaids. I like the attempt at a more passive storytelling approach, as story is largely done via optional NPC interactions. Someone truly looking to engage with everything the game has to offer will be able to find out either the direction they need to go with the story or where secrets are. Much of navigating what needs to happen next is through talking to NPCs, which I personally did not mind all too much.


Gameplay: 2/5

Good Mechanics

I think the game does a good job of rewarding the player for finding and understanding the strengths and weaknesses of certain enemies, and rewards a balanced playstyle. A big example of this is enemies like Black Flan. Fairly unassuming, but takes virtually no damage from physical hits. Other enemies like some bosses take reduced damage from elemental attacks, physical attacks, or magic, and the onus is placed on the player to navigate this mid-fight. For subsequent Final Fantasy games, this isn't a praise I would give, but for a pioneering game such as this, I feel it deserves credit.

Worse Mechanics

  • Overall gigantic lack of clarity on game systems. Accuracy is not accuracy as it is in like 99% of JRPGs. My black mage not being able to learn several spells from magic shops is bewildering. The class upgrade is cool in concept, but being able to learn some lvl 5/6 black magic and not others will leave players understandably confused. Same goes for unequipable armor and weapons.
  • The magic points system fundementally discourages magic use. Before endgame where at level 50 you get 9 points in each magic level, you will often be stuck at something like 2-4 points in whatever the most powerful spell is, especially apparent for black magic. With how gargantuan the dungeons in FFI are, unless you stock up on 99 ethers, you will blow through these before finishing the first floor. This leads to a "I better save my MP for later" attitude which persists *through bossfights*. This is not satisfying. The similar argument could be made for a true MP system where characters have a magic bar as well, but oftentimes spells are less powerful, whereas a black mages 1 shots most enemies at nearly all parts of the game.
  • Red mage hp bar is a constant frustration and seems unintuitively low. Don't misunderstand, the red mage is a perfectly strong character, and in concept a small health bar seems like a reasonable way to balance having what is essentially a warrior, black mage AND white mage in one character. The end result seems a bit extreme, as the red mage in *practice* is much weaker than the warrior offensively until masamune can be equipped...in the final dungeon. The black magic capabilities are weak, white magic usage is a nice QoL for saving white mage MP and applying Protera buffs. I do not think this constitutes the red mage having what seems like 70% of the HP of a white/black mage for much of the game.

Dungeons: 2/5

Dungeons are a bit rough in FFI, and it should probably be a 1/5. The primary issue is that they are simply too long and too empty. Some of these (Volcano, looking at you) are just unbelievably large and takes AGES to traverse with all the encounters. Take this into account with the lack of save points, and every dungeon feels like a mind numbing chore, as absolutely nothing interesting is happening all the while. The only dungeon that attempted to mix this up was the ice cave with its collapsing floors, which was a fun touch to trial and error the way to the Levistone. Another point against this game's dungons is the frequency of monster chests. Monster chests are a fun way to break up potential dungeon monotony, where suddenly a monster chest with a powerful enemy can catch a non-fully healed up party off guard, leading to a tense encounter. If you make 40-60% of the chests monster chests...it is no longer a clever subversion of expectations. Especially when all of the enemies (looking at you once again Volcano) are the SAME ENCOUNTER. In the Earth Shrine, there is at least a dozen chest encounters, all of which are two Earth Elementals which are pitifully easy to defeat. If the mechanic to break monotony becomes the norm, then it is not really doing its job effectively. Boss designs are passable, the cardinal sin of the FFI bosses is how forgettable they are. Each of the 4 fiends are a mostly dull battle, the only one posing any real threat is Tiamat, and they pose little to no threat to any party who has already obtained the class upgrade and can use lvl 8 black magic (Flare). The re-fights in the Chaos Shrine are fun, but I discovered that I was painfully underlevelled. I imagine if I had been at the appropriate level, they would have been a breeze to defeat. the final boss, Chaos is I believe a bit frustratingly designed due to his heal mechanic. at 20k health, Chaos takes a while to whittle down. Theres a small but random chance that he heals 10k health on his turn, which undoes around 10-12 of your own turns (unless you are very optimized for the fight). This makes the fight a bit RNG reliant which is never a good thing.


Innovation: 3/5

Not too many notes to say here, while certainly rough around the edges, the game kicked off a massive JRPG franchise which speaks for itself in many ways. The job system, as well as the battles with a party of ~4 members which can be seen in countless games today.


Characters: 1/5

The characters are the weakest point of the game. They are the combination of all the worst features of final fantasy characters:

  • Job system = no personality. The 4 warriors are 100% blank slate characters who do not have any indistinguishable features except job and name (which has no default even in this game). No interactions with any NPCs except for as a group, no talking at all, simply dull characterization.
  • No backstory of any kind. The game introduced them as the 4 warriors of light and then...that is all we know about them for the *entire rest of the game.*

Final Fantasy is a flawed game that certainly did not age well, but having played dozens of JRPGs and similar games, experiencing the origins of several of those mechanics was enjoyable nonetheless.
